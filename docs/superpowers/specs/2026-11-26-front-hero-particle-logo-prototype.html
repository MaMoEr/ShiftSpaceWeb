Front-page hero · four concepts

Where two worlds meet, in real depth

Each concept is a live three.js scene built from code, in brand colors, with the site's real header and headline on top. The mouse nudges it and scrolling drives it forward. Nothing has to be won or solved; it's something to watch. These are rough prototypes to react to, not finished heroes.

01Strong

The Portal

The ShiftSpace mark is a huge amber ring floating in a dark tech space. Through it you see a bright, warm, playful world with real depth.

Mouse
Moves the camera, so you look past the edge of the ring and see more of the other world.
Scroll
Flies you through the ring into the other world.
Two worlds
Shown literally: a cold, precise tech world outside, play and imagination inside, and the logo is the door between them.
To finish
A properly modeled mark from Blender (.glb), a designed inner world (planes, shapes from your projects) and tuned lighting. Medium effort.
02Strong

The Seam

The logo's diagonal stroke cuts the scene in two. On one side everything is a glowing blueprint; on the other, the same objects are glossy, colorful toys.

Mouse
The seam follows the cursor. Wherever it passes, objects switch between blueprint and toy.
Scroll
Sweeps the seam across the whole scene until everything is "real".
Two worlds
Tech meets play in one image. The ring plus the seam draws the logo itself.
To finish
Replace the shapes with objects that mean something (a paper plane, a square, a phone, an animal). Medium effort, low risk; it runs well on phones.
03

The Swarm

The ShiftSpace mark as a glowing volume of particles. It bursts apart into a drifting cloud and pulls itself back together.

Mouse
Pushes the particles aside; they flow back into shape.
Scroll
Scatters the mark; scrolling back up re-forms it.
Two worlds
Loose digital matter that finds its form. Other shapes are parked for now.
To finish
Move the simulation to the GPU for 200k+ particles and sharper shapes. Medium effort.
04

The Lifted Place

A small town square, lifted into the air like a diorama at night. A digital layer comes alive above it: holograms, a scan line, AR labels, glowing creatures.

Mouse
Turns and tilts the view around the island.
Scroll
The island rises and the camera moves closer.
Two worlds
Closest to what you actually sell: a real place that wakes up. The headline "Lyfter rummet. Väcker platsen." becomes literal.
To finish
This one needs Blender most: a modeled square (or a stylized Härnösand). Largest effort.

Scroll on the front page

Today the hero and footer fit exactly one screen, so there is nothing to scroll. A scroll-driven hero needs scroll room (as here) or a short section below. The mouse alone is enough to make every concept work.

Weight and phones

three.js is about 170 KB gzipped from a CDN. Each scene drops its resolution and particle count on phones, and a still poster image covers slow devices, no WebGL and reduced motion.

Headline and header

The header and headline are untouched. The scene sits underneath, and the headline gets the same dark shadow behind it as the current image.

Blender's role

The prototypes use simple shapes. For the chosen concept, a modeled logo mark and objects exported as .glb files lift the quality the most.
